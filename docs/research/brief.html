Where recognisers fail on Arabic script, and why

The findings that matter from our measurements, with their numbers, and how each relates to the papers we have read. The full evidence is in the ledger.

ب ت ثone skeleton, three dot patterns

Measurement firstMany apparent model gaps were crops, conventions, leaked lines or bugs. We fixed those before comparing models.

Skeletons, not dotsMost recognition error is a misread letter skeleton. Dots, spaces and language each add a smaller cost.

Shape noveltyThe more unusual a letter's shape compared with training, the more likely it is misread, in every dataset.

Language must obey the imageA free-running language model hurts. Priors only help when the image constrains them.

Are the numbers right?A

Cropping the line to its ink is the biggest single gain

KHATT 31.0 → 23.3 % CER

All 14 CRNNs land at 22–24 % after cropping, with no retraining. Base VLMs don't care, but three fine-tuned VLMs lose 3–4 points on loose crops. The full KHATT set is already tight, so the crop changes nothing there.

Shapes and dotsA

Unusual letter shapes predict errors best

error 1–5 % → 30–53 %

Letter error climbs from the most typical fifth of shapes to the most novel band in every dataset, and survives the confound checks (odds ratio about 16 for CRNNs, 4.4 for fine-tuned VLMs).

Adding missing shapes helps only where they are missing

−0.8 points [−1.6, +0.1]

Shape-targeted repeats beat a random control on held-out KHATT lines, but not on NAKBA, where the gain is plain repetition.

Shapes and dotsA

Skeletons, not dots, are the problem

≈ 90 % of error survives without dots

Lines that are right except for their dots are rare (0.1–4 %). The dot tax is a median 1.8 CER points, 8 % of error.

Alshaibani 2025 · dotless Arabic
Shapes and dotsB

Teeth are the weak spot

+6–21 % over baseline, 6 of 6 families

Letters with teeth are misread more than their letter baseline in every model family. Among one-tooth letters, error rises with run length.

Shapes and dotsB

Confusions are visual, and shared by every model family

ρ 0.63–0.90 between families

Pairs that differ only in dots are 3–18 times more confusable than other pairs. Models confuse letters that look alike, not letters that sound alike.

Müller 2012 · letter identification

Dots return from context for known words, not for unseen ones

95 % restored by the top candidate

A quarter to a third of words share their dotless form with another word, yet the most frequent word restores 95 % of the known ones.

Spacing and geometryB

Word gaps and word-part gaps look alike

P = 0.68 (CRNN), 0.63 (VLMs)

Models insert spaces inside words at part gaps far more than chance (87 of 89 pairs). The space tax is 2–11 CER points against 1–4 for dots.

A free-running LLM decoder makes recognition worse

KHATT 26.4 → 40.0 %

NAKBA goes 13.8 → 20.8 %, and a model three times larger is no better. Fluent but wrong words are 41–44 % of errors, against 24 % for the CRNN.

R² 0.152 vs 0.088

KHATT is one fixed paragraph, so it works as a natural experiment. For the KHATT-trained model it flips: writer 0.26, text 0.03.

Models look at the right letters and still misread them

about 3× chance

Attribution maps point at a token's own letters three times above chance, but wrong tokens look in the right place as often as correct ones.
