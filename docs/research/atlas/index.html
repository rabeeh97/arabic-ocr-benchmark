2 · Framework

What we measure, and why

A characteristic stays on this list only if it can change a decision about D1 data, D2 model, D3 transformations (normalisation, pre- and post-processing) or D4 evaluation. It counts as validated once it predicts model errors on held-out datasets and models, and a controlled change reproduces the effect.

Validation gates

  1. V1 Reliable. Deterministic and unit-tested, with tight bootstrap intervals.
  2. V2 Predictive. Explains per-sample error beyond line length, and holds when a dataset or model family is left out.
  3. V3 Causal. A synthetic or natural single-factor change reproduces the effect.
  4. V4 Generalises. Works unchanged on Persian, Urdu and Pashto, and reproduces known Latin results.
  5. V5 Useful. Acting on it changes an outcome: a ranking, a conclusion, or the data we choose.
  6. V6 Adoptable. Released as a library, datasheet template and this page.

Full reasoning and prior art: docs/research/characteristics-framework.md

3 · Method, on real lines

How a letter's locus is extracted

Every Arabic letter is drawn in up to four shapes, chosen by whether it connects to the letter before it and the letter after it. We recover the intended shape from the transcription the same way a text renderer does. These are the forms the writer was meant to draw. Handwriting sometimes breaks or adds a join.

Clean the encoding

Strip invisible bidi and zero-width characters, turn presentation-form code points back into plain letters, and apply Unicode NFC so combining marks sit in canonical order.

Read each joining type

From Unicode's ArabicShaping.txt: D joins both sides (ب ج س …), R joins only to the letter before it (ا د ذ ر ز و ة), U never joins (ء), C is tatweel, and diacritics are T, transparent, so they are skipped.

Decide the form

A letter joins backward if the previous letter is D or C and it is D or R itself. It joins forward if it is D and the next letter is D, R or C.

Map to the skeleton

Each (letter, form) maps to one of 19 dotless skeletons, the rasm. The map depends on position: initial ن and ي share ب's tooth, but their final forms have their own shapes. A letter is dot-ambiguous if another letter shares its skeleton at that position.

Cut word pieces

A new PAW (piece of Arabic word) starts at every letter that does not join backward. دار is three PAWs; بيت is one.
