Summary

Where we stand

We set out to find the best achievable RASAM score and to test whether better data gives better results. We ended with a small, fully documented recogniser that is second only to Calfa's unpublished models on the official test lines, and with measurements showing that most published RASAM numbers describe reading inside known manuscripts, not new ones.

Findings

What we found

  1. No two published RASAM numbers are on the same test lines new

    Every paper before 2026 made its own split, and none is described well enough to reproduce. Only Calfa's AMIDDA paper scores on the official Hugging Face test lines: its test split contains all 734 / 372 / 229 official images, identical by MD5 (our check). That makes Calfa's per-corpus CTC numbers the only published ones directly comparable to ours.

  2. The random line split flatters RASAM-2 by about half again new

    Leave-manuscript-out over all 15 RASAM-2 manuscripts (5 folds of 3): on the same official test lines a model that never saw the manuscript reads 9.87 % CER against 6.69 % for the same recipe trained on the random split (MS 1936 excluded). That is +3.2 points, +48 % relative. All 14 regular manuscripts get worse (+0.16 to +7.09 points). This is the first measurement of that gap on RASAM.

  3. The gap is larger across collections new

    On the four AraMS-28k Maghrebi books, which no model of ours has seen, CER is 6.2 to 20.9 % (16.8 % pooled), against 4 to 9 % on RASAM's own test lines and 12.2 % on unseen RASAM-2 manuscripts.

  4. Maghrebi training data beats more mixed-script data new

    On AraMS's own Maghrebi test book our model, trained only on about 11k RASAM and TariMa lines, reads 13.94 % CER. The AraMS paper reports 32.71 % (Kraken) and 37.88 % (HATFormer) for models trained on AraMS's 19.7k mixed-script training lines. Only 4.3 % of that book's lines share a five-word phrase with our training text, so the LM is not recalling known text.

  5. Two "Maghrebi" AraMS books use eastern fa / qaf dotting new

    Our model reads 19.2 % (book_20) and 15.4 % (book_24) of their fa as qaf, against about 0.5 % in the other two books; book_24 also has 296 qaf read as ta. In Maghrebi script fa has its dot below and qaf one dot above, so a Maghrebi reader takes an eastern fa for qaf. Books 19 and 20 copy the same text (the Muwatta) and score 6.2 vs 19.7 %, so the hand decides, not the text.

  6. Public models are contaminated or crop-sensitive new

    HAFITH has 362 of the 734 official RASAM-1 test lines in its training or validation data; on the 372 clean lines it scores 12.34 % (its overall 6.27 %, self-reported 4.95 %). Phoenix reports 7.67 % on its own split but reads the official crops at 23.1 %. Calfa's AMIDDA VLM reproduces close to its card (21.7 / 28.3 / 24.8 % vs 25.0 / 27.6 / 24.2 %).

  7. The data has defects that move scores new

    183 of the 250 published MS 1936 crops (RASAM-2) are broken: 80 are 1 to 7 px tall and 103 were cut from the wrong place on the page (other lines, the library stamp, bare paper), although the PAGE-XML polygons are right. Only 3 of its 22 test crops are correct. Re-cutting them from the pages takes RASAM-2 test CER from 10.79 to 7.81 % (greedy, same model). Some RASAM-1 references are uncorrected HTR output: MS 1977 pages 0017, 0071 and 0158 were reported in 2021 (calfa-co/rasam-dataset#2) and never fixed. Others are shifted by one line, repeat text, or miss the end of the line.

  8. Diversity beats volume verified

    Adding RASAM-1 to RASAM-2 training cut RASAM-2 error by a third (18.47 → 12.84 % greedy). Adding about 1,800 lines from 26 new TariMa documents improved every test set. Adding about 1,470 more lines from the three RASAM-1 manuscripts the model already knew did nothing.

  9. Calfa's lead is not explained by anything they published negative

    Per-corpus specialisation, keeping more vertical detail, and Calfa's own described architecture (52.9 M parameters, trained as long as it kept improving) all ended at or behind our 2.4 M model. The remaining candidates are unpublished training details or training data that overlapped today's test pages. A question to Calfa is drafted and not yet sent.

  10. Cheap decoding gains are real; weight averaging is not verified

    A character 8-gram LM in the CTC beam search gives −0.4 to −2.5 points; AraGPT2 rescoring of the 10-best adds −0.2 to −0.3 on every set. EMA and checkpoint averaging give nothing over the one-cycle schedule. Seed noise is 0.1 to 0.2 points. A two-model ensemble at the hypothesis level (pool both models' 10-best lists and score them with both models and the LMs) is the best system so far: 3.72 / 7.71 / 5.67 %. Averaging the two models' frame outputs instead makes things worse.

  11. Cleaning the training data did not help; cleaning the test data did negative

    We re-cut 250 crops from the page images and dropped 202 lines (161 train, 20 validation, 21 test) by automatic rules plus the manual check proposed in calfa-co/rasam-dataset PR #7. The same 60-epoch recipe retrained on the clean training set is within seed noise of the original on every test view (clean test: 4.19 / 7.65 / 8.39 % vs 4.24 / 7.79 / 8.24 %). About 2 % bad training references do not hurt a CTC model measurably. The fixes to the test set do change the scores: RASAM-2 10.8 → 7.8 %, RASAM-1 4.8 → 4.2 % (greedy).

  12. On the cleaned test lines our best system reads 3.17 / 5.47 / 5.59 % new

    Re-cutting the 25 broken test crops and removing the 21 lines with wrong references (see Clean benchmark) lowers every system. The RASAM-2 gap to Calfa's reported 4.9 % shrinks from 2.8 to 0.6 points, and the ranking does not change. Page-bootstrap 95 % intervals and paired differences are given for every system we can run.

  13. A 7B Arabic VLM fine-tune halves AMIDDA's error new

    On the same 200 official test lines per set, our light LoRA fine-tune of Fanar-2-Oryx 7B (1 epoch, vision frozen, about 5k RASAM lines in a 22k mixed set) reads 11.2 / 17.2 % against 22.7 / 28.8 % for Calfa's AMIDDA 1.0 Line 4B. Our same-size Qwen3-VL-4B fine-tune trails AMIDDA (27.3 / 32.1 %). Every VLM is still far behind the 2.4 M-parameter CRNN.

Clean benchmark

The official test lines, cleaned, with error bars

The same official test lines, scored three ways for every system we can run:

  • Official: the lines as published.
  • Clean: the 25 broken test crops (23 RASAM-2, 2 RASAM-1) are replaced by crops re-cut from the page, and every system re-reads them. The 21 test lines whose reference does not match the image are removed.
  • People-found drops only: the same re-cuts, but only lines flagged by people are removed (the manual check in PR #7 and our by-eye list). This guards against our automatic rules, which come from CRNN readings, favouring CRNN-like readers.

Small grey numbers are 95 % intervals from resampling whole pages, because lines on one page share a hand and are not independent. Systems we cannot run (Calfa CTC, Gemini) have no clean number.

{{CLEANBENCH}}

What changes:

  • Our best system goes from 3.72 / 7.71 / 5.67 % to 3.17 / 5.47 / 5.59 %.
  • On RASAM-2, the re-cuts alone remove more than 2 points and shrink the interval from 5.8–10.0 to 4.7–6.4: the broken MS 1936 crops were the main source of uncertainty.

Interpretation

Why the scores differ, and why papers used their own splits

Why scores differ

  • Different test lines. Each paper scored its own sample. RASAM-2's 15 manuscripts vary widely in difficulty; 7 broken MS 1936 crops alone hold 12 % of our RASAM-2 errors.
  • Seen vs new manuscripts (measured). On the same lines, an unseen manuscript costs +3.2 points on RASAM-2 (F2). Every random-split number measures reading inside known manuscripts.
  • Leakage. The HF split is random by line: every test page also has lines in train. HAFITH's own split even holds half the official RASAM-1 test lines in training.
  • Different references. Standardising the references alone moves our RASAM-1 score from 4.32 to 3.95 %.
  • Different averaging. Micro, per-line and per-page CER differ by about 1 point on the same predictions (6.82 % micro vs 7.57 % mean per page vs 5.71 % median per page for one model). Most papers do not say which they use.
  • Different crops. Kraken models expect their own binarised polygon crops: Phoenix reads 73.8 % on raw HF crops and 13.6 % on the same lines binarised.
  • Different training data. Per-corpus specialised models beat generic multi-script models on RASAM by a wide margin.

Why papers used their own splits

  • The 2021, 2022 and 2024 RASAM papers predate the Hugging Face splits (created 2026-05-20). The GitHub release had no split, so each paper made one.
  • Phoenix chose a manuscript-independent split on purpose, arguing that random line splits overstate generalisation. Our leave-manuscript-out result confirms that argument with a number.
  • HAFITH built one combined benchmark across Muharaf, KHATT and RASAM with its own 60/20/20 split, which overlaps the official test lines.
  • Calfa's AMIDDA paper (2026) is the first to report on the official lines, which is why it is the only published entry on the strict board.
Our model

How our recogniser got here

A character CRNN with CTC (VGG-style CNN, 3-layer BiLSTM, width 0.5, 2.4 M parameters), trained on the official train splits, chosen on validation, and decoded with a character 8-gram LM in a prefix beam search. The final recipe trains on RASAM-1, repaired RASAM-2 and TariMa at 96 px for 60 epochs and rescores the 10-best with AraGPT2.

What the errors are (RASAM-1, early model)

  • Word spacing, 16 % of errors. Gaps between letter groups and between words look alike in cursive script.
  • One-dot confusions, 12 % of errors. The Maghrebi pairs lead: ب→ف and ن→ق, because Maghrebi fa has its dot below (like ba) and qaf one dot above (like nun).
  • Thin letters lost or invented, about 12 %. ل م ا at 64 px; the move to 96 px removed about a sixth of RASAM-1 errors.
  • Fragments. Catchwords 51–60 %, marginalia about 8–16 %, very short lines worst.

Cleaning

Fix what can be fixed, drop the rest, retrain

No hand edits.
