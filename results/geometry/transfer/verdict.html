Run on the 3090 CRNN: making KHATT look like Muharaf lowers its CER by 1.1 points (26.3 % to 25.3 %, 95 % interval −1.7 to −0.4), and the whole effect comes from slant. The reverse confirms it: making Muharaf look like KHATT raises its CER from 6.2 % to 8.2 % (+2.0, interval +1.5 to +2.4), again all from slant. Tone, paper noise and stroke width do nothing for KHATT; for NAKBA, thinning the strokes to Muharaf’s width gains 1.1 points and removing ruled lines changes nothing. So the model is sensitive to slant, but slant explains only about 1 of the 20 points between KHATT and Muharaf; the rest is letter shape, writers and text, and KHATT is also the farthest set in text (next sections).
