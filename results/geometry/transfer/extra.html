Positive shear leans the strokes further right, toward Muharaf. On KHATT every rightward shear helps a little (best +12°, 26.0 %), and keeping the most confident of the five readings gives 25.4 %, 0.9 points better than the plain reading. On NAKBA the plain reading is already best and confidence selection does not help. The oracle shows that a perfect selector would gain 1.4 to 3.1 points, so the confidence signal captures only part of what the variants offer. Code: scripts/xai_geom_tta.py.

The other side of the gap: text

The transformation leaves letter shapes and text alone, so whatever it does not explain lies there. Measured against the CRNN’s training text (all Muharaf train labels plus RASAM), KHATT is also the most distant set in language, not only in slant:

No set has characters the model cannot output, and rare letter shapes are negligible everywhere. KHATT’s text is about 0.6 bits per character less predictable than NAKBA’s and has about twice the share of unseen PAWs. The BiLSTM in the CRNN learns some of this context, so part of the remaining KHATT gap is plausibly the language model inside the recogniser, and part is writers and letter shapes. What this changes

  • Slant is a real sensitivity, not the main cause. Shearing Muharaf to KHATT’s upright slant raises the 3090 CRNN’s CER by a third (6.2 % to 8.2 %), but undoing KHATT’s slant recovers only 1.1 of its 20 points. Slant augmentation in training (shear up to about +20° and down to −12°) is cheap and should remove this sensitivity; it is worth adding to the next CRNN and VLM fine-tunes.
  • Tone and paper do not matter for this model, because it normalises each line’s grey levels; ruled lines in NAKBA cost nothing measurable.
  • Stroke weight matters for NAKBA (thinning its strokes to Muharaf’s width gains 1.1 points), which points at stroke-width augmentation or normalization.
  • The bigger lever for KHATT is elsewhere: writers and letter shapes (the allograph library, X-G10) and text (lexicon or language-model adaptation), which is also where the E8 encoder-to-LLM spike would act.
